📋 DU Semester 1 Syllabus Overview

The first semester of BSc Life Science (Prog.) follows the UGCF 2022 syllabus and brings together Botany, Chemistry and Zoology. The core papers (DSCs) are 4 credits each, with 2 theory and 2 practical:

🌿 (BOT-DSC-1) Plant Diversity & Systematics: Diversity of life, microbes, algae, fungi, bryophytes, pteridophytes, gymnosperms, angiosperms, systematics and systems of classification.
⚗️ (CHEM-DSC-01) Basic Concepts of Organic Chemistry: Fundamentals of organic chemistry, stereochemistry and the main types of organic reactions.
🐾 (ZOO-DSC-01) Diversity of Animals: Introduction, Protista to pseudocoelomates, coelomates and chordates.

📈 How to Study for a 9+ CGPA in BSc Life Science

A strong CGPA in BSc Life Science isn't really about reading more. It's about studying in the right order across three subjects. Here is a method that works for semester exams.

1

Start with the syllabus

Read the unit list for each paper first. The lecture hours show where the weight sits: for example, Organic Chemistry Unit 3 is 18 of 30 lectures. Once PYQs are added to this page, use them to see which topics repeat.

2

Study one unit properly with the notes

Pick one unit and read it through instead of jumping to definitions. Understanding a concept once makes it far easier to recall in the exam than memorising it without context.

3

Practise every diagram, life cycle and mechanism

Botany has life cycles, Zoology has labelled diagrams and Chemistry has reaction mechanisms. Draw or write each one by hand a few times instead of only looking at it.

4

Test yourself from memory

After finishing a unit, close the notes and try to write the key points, diagrams and mechanisms from memory. Whatever you can't recall is what to revise next.

5

Write full answers under a timer, at least once

For long-answer questions, practise writing a complete answer within a set time. It teaches you to structure and finish an answer in time, which matters in the exam hall.

6

Rotate across the three subjects

All three papers carry 4 credits, so don't leave one for last. Finish one unit at a time, and switch between Botany, Chemistry and Zoology through the week.

💡 Use the syllabus, the notes and your own practice together instead of separately, and give diagrams and mechanisms the same attention as written answers.
